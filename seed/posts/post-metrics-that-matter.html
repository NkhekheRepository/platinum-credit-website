Metrics are supposed to clarify decisions. In many companies, they do the opposite: dashboards bristle with numbers while leadership argues about which ones matter. The fix is ruthless prioritization of what gets tracked.

Fewer metrics, better decisions

A metric earns its place by changing a decision. If a number cannot influence an upcoming choice, it is decoration. Start from the decisions you actually face this quarter, and work backwards to the handful of metrics that inform them.

A practical rule set

  • One or two north-star metrics per business line.
  • Each metric has a named owner and a stated target.
  • Reviews ask "what would we do differently?", not "why did the number change?".
  • Retire metrics that have not changed a decision in two quarters.

Done right, a dashboard is a conversation tool, not a reporting chore. Five numbers you act on beat fifty you admire.
