Most strategy documents fail long before execution. They are written in boardroom language, weight fifty pages, and describe a future state no single person is accountable for. If you have a shelf full of reports nobody acts on, you have experienced this firsthand.

The gap is usually process, not ambition

When a strategy stalls, we rarely find that the ambition was wrong. More often, the operating model cannot carry it: decisions have no owner, hand-offs have no deadlines, and success has no definition.

The fix is not another vision workshop. It is a small number of concrete changes — who decides, who does, who checks, and what "done" looks like.

A plan you can execute

  • Pick three outcomes for the quarter, not twelve for the year.
  • Name one accountable owner per outcome.
  • Define the metric and the review cadence before you start.
  • Write the plan in language your frontline managers use.

Strategy is only valuable when it changes what happens on Monday. If a plan is too long to read, it is too long to work.
