Software projects fail for many reasons: weak requirements, poor data, vendor lock-in. But if you study the post-mortems, the dominant cause is adoption. The tool works; the people quietly keep working the old way.

Adoption is a deliverable, not a hope

Too many rollouts treat training as the final week before go-live. By then, resistance has solidified and the "old system" has become an identity. Adoption must be designed from the first week.

  • Map the new tool to existing workflows — never the reverse.
  • Involve day-to-day operators in configuration decisions.
  • Pilot with a willing team, then expand with their proof points.
  • Train in role-based cohorts, not whole-company sessions.

Measure adoption like a metric

Track active usage, not just logins. Compare cycle times before and after. If the numbers have not moved within sixty days, the rollout is not over — the change management is under-resourced.

A tool is a multiplier. It only multiplies what people actually do. Invest at least as much in the people side as in the software, and the numbers will follow.
